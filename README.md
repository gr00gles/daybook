# Daybook

A Chrome new tab page that shows the time, the weather (with severe weather alerts), your Google and Outlook calendars with when to leave for each event, unread Gmail and Outlook mail, your deliveries, pages for the companies you deal with, the latest YouTube uploads, stocks, your teams' next games, new New York State job postings, headlines, a search bar, your most-visited sites and a notes pad.

It is a small Chrome extension. Everything runs in the browser; there is no server.

## Install (for anyone you share it with)

1. Unzip `daybook.zip` and move the `daybook` folder somewhere permanent, such as Documents. Chrome loads the extension from that folder, so don't delete it.
2. Open `chrome://extensions` and turn on **Developer mode** (top right).
3. Click **Load unpacked** and choose the `daybook` folder.
4. Open a new tab. If Chrome asks whether to keep the new tab page, choose **Keep it**.
5. Daybook's setup screen walks through three steps:
   - **Connect Google.** Click *Sign in with Google*. Chrome may ask you to sign in to Chrome with your Google account. Google then says *"Google hasn't verified this app"*: click **Advanced**, then **Go to Daybook**, tick the calendar box and click **Continue**. Daybook also finds every Gmail account signed into Chrome; if none is, click *Sign in to Gmail*.
   - **Connect Outlook** (optional). Click *Sign in with Microsoft* and accept. If Microsoft asks *"Stay signed in?"*, choose **Yes**. Work or school accounts often need IT approval first; Daybook gives you a link to send them.
   - **Make it yours.** Name, °F or °C, weather location, headline topics.

You can change all of this later with the gear icon, or rerun setup from Settings → Accounts.

## For the owner: what's set up and how to keep it working

Sign-in works for everyone because this copy carries two app IDs you registered once:

- **Google:** the `oauth2.client_id` in `manifest.json` (Google Cloud project "Daybook", Chrome Extension client, Item ID `flgomcdbgonicihokfkbpopedcilcpdn`).
- **Microsoft:** `MICROSOFT_CLIENT_ID` in `config.js` (Entra app registration "Daybook", SPA redirect `https://flgomcdbgonicihokfkbpopedcilcpdn.chromiumapp.org/`).

Things to know:

- **Never change the `key` in `manifest.json`.** It fixes the extension ID, and both app registrations are tied to that ID.
- **Google Cloud APIs to keep enabled:** Google Calendar API, Gmail API (mark as read/delete only) and YouTube Data API v3 (YouTube card). Scopes on the Data Access page: `calendar.readonly`, `calendar.events` (adding events), `userinfo.email`, `userinfo.profile`, `gmail.modify`, `youtube.readonly`.
- **Google publishing status must be "In production"**, not "Testing". In Testing, only listed test users can sign in and everyone has to sign in again every 7 days. Publishing needs the home page and privacy policy at `https://gr00gles.github.io/`.
- **The Google app is unverified, so it can be used by at most 100 different Google accounts, ever.** That's plenty for friends and family. Going past it would require Google's verification review.
- **Keep both registrations alive.** Google deletes OAuth clients nobody has used for 6 months, and Microsoft may delete an unused directory. Using Daybook yourself keeps the Google client active; sign in to entra.microsoft.com every few months.
- **Gmail** is read through Gmail's inbox feed using the browser's own Gmail sign-in, so it needs no permission screen. Google doesn't officially document this feed for personal accounts, so it could change someday.

## Sharing it

Zip the `daybook` folder (or reuse `daybook.zip`) and send it. Each person installs it as above and signs in with their own accounts; nobody sees anyone else's data.

## Troubleshooting

- **"Google hasn't verified this app" with no Advanced link:** the person's Google account is a work or school account whose admin blocks unverified apps. Personal @gmail.com accounts work.
- **Google sign-in does nothing:** Chrome sign-in may be turned off (Chrome settings → You and Google → *Allow Chrome sign-in*), or the window is incognito.
- **Gmail shows nothing:** make sure you're signed in to Gmail in Chrome, and that Daybook's *Site access* in `chrome://extensions` → Details is *On all sites*.
- **Outlook asks to reconnect now and then:** Microsoft limits browser apps to a day at a time and renews quietly using your Microsoft sign-in. If you signed out of Outlook.com or cleared cookies, click *Reconnect Outlook*.

## Using it

- **Unread** mail: click a message to read it right on the page. **Open in Gmail** (or **Outlook**) takes you to the full site, and Ctrl-click or ⌘-click still opens it there directly. Opening a message marks it read, as Gmail does; **Keep unread** undoes that. Settings → Accounts picks the folders to show unread mail from: for Gmail, the whole inbox, any of its tabs (Primary, Social, Promotions, Updates, Forums), Important, Starred and labels of your own by name; for Outlook, any of your top-level folders. Pictures from the web stay hidden until you click **Show pictures**, so senders can't tell you opened their email. Whole messages can be read for Outlook and for the Google account connected in Settings; for other signed-in Gmail accounts you see the preview and the Open in Gmail button. The first time, Google asks for permission.
- Hover over a message to **mark it as read**, **junk** it (Gmail's Report spam, or Outlook's Junk Email folder) or **delete** it (it goes to Trash or Deleted Items). The reader has the same Junk and Delete buttons. A bar at the bottom offers **Undo**. The first time, Google or Microsoft asks you to allow it. For Gmail this works on the Google account connected in Settings.
- **YouTube** flips through the latest uploads from the channels you subscribe to, one at a time, every few seconds (hover to hold it still; use the arrows, scroll wheel or arrow keys to flip). Videos from the last 24 hours are tagged **New**. Hover over a video and click the **eye** to mark it as watched and take it out of the stack (Undo is at the bottom); opening a video does the same. YouTube doesn't let apps see your watch history, so this list is Daybook's own, kept on this computer. Click **Show my subscriptions** once to allow it. Shorts are hidden unless you turn that off in Settings → YouTube.
- **Headlines** shows the topic you pick with the tabs beside its title: the top story large, then as many more as fit, each with its source's icon and how long ago it ran. They can be in the ticker too (below).
- **Arrange cards** with the grid button at the top right (the arrange, refresh and settings buttons sit small and stacked at the end of the top bar): drag a card by its handle to move it to any spot or column (or focus the handle and use the arrow keys), drag its bottom edge to make it shorter or taller (the cards under it make room; double-click the edge to go back, and with the edge focused, ↑ and ↓ step it), and drag the lines between columns to widen them. **Columns 3 / 4** in the bar switches the board between three columns and four; wide windows start with four, and each keeps its own arrangement. Click **Done** when finished; **Reset layout** puts everything back to the default board, including which cards are in the flip card. The layout syncs with your Chrome profile.
- **Everything on one screen:** the board starts with Today and NYS jobs; the month calendar and Markets; and Unread, the flip card (which takes turns showing Headlines, Notes, Around you and YouTube), Deliveries and Teams. With four columns, the flip card and Deliveries get a column of their own, and Teams sits under Unread. Cards are a fixed size: while the columns sit side by side they fill the window between them, and a card never grows or shrinks as what's in it changes, with one exception: Unread and Teams in the same column share their room by how much mail there is, so Teams grows as the unread count goes down and shrinks as it goes up, always showing at least two teams. A card with more than fits shows as many whole emails, events, deliveries or rows as it can, never a scroll bar. A few keep a minimum, taking the room from the cards beside them (Markets gets shorter, say): the month calendar always shows up to three events on each day, NYS jobs at least two postings and Deliveries at least four packages. In narrow windows, or with **Fit to window** turned off in the arrange bar, every card keeps a set height and the page scrolls.
- Cards with nothing to show, such as an empty inbox, say so in a single line at the top.
- **Today** lists today's events, with a timeline and Join buttons for video calls, plus the next six days as room allows. Next to it, the **month calendar** shows every event for the month, with events that last several days drawn as one bar across them; use the arrows to flip months. Click a day and the Today card shows that day instead (its holidays too), with the days after it; the month stays as it is. Click the day again, or **Back to today** on the Today card, to go back.
- **Event details:** click anywhere on an event (in Today, or in a day's schedule under the month calendar) to open its details: the time, calendar, place with Map and Directions links, the forecast and leave-by time, the video call, notes and guests. On a Google calendar you can edit, or your Outlook calendar, **Edit** changes the title, time, place or notes; for a repeating event it changes that one only. **Delete** removes it after you confirm (again just that one of a series). A bar at the bottom offers **Undo**; for Outlook, Undo after a delete adds the event back as a copy, and isn't offered when others were invited. Invitations from someone else, and calendars added by iCal link, can only be read.
- **Add an event** with the **+** that shows at the top right of the Today card when you point at it (it adds to the day the card shows), or by double-clicking a day on the month calendar. Pick which calendar it goes on: any Google calendar you can edit, or your main Outlook calendar. Google calendars can get a Google Meet link. The Location field suggests addresses as you type (your recent places first, then the nearest matches from Photon, no key needed). The first time, Google or Microsoft asks you to allow adding events. A bar at the bottom offers **Undo**, which deletes the event again. Calendars added by iCal link are read-only.
- **Travel time:** events with an address show the forecast there at that hour and, for the next 24 hours, a **Leave by** time from where you are (drive time plus 5 minutes, which you can change in Settings → Calendar). The **Next** bubble at the end of the search box counts down to leaving. Drive times come from OSRM, which doesn't know about traffic; paste a free TomTom key in Settings → Calendar to use live traffic instead. Video calls, rooms and places more than 250 km away are skipped.
- **Weather** sits at the top right: today's conditions and the next six hours (in windows wide enough for them). Click it for **This week**, a bubble with the sun's day (sunrise to sunset, with the sun where it is now) and the moon, the hourly chart, the 7-day forecast and details like wind, UV and air quality (click outside it or press Esc to close).
- **Weather alerts** from the US National Weather Service show as a chip above the current conditions; click it for the full text in the week bubble. Outside the US there are none.
- **What's next and the next holiday** share a bubble at the end of the search box, flipping over to the other every few seconds: your next event in its calendar's color, with its time, when to leave and a Join button, and the next holiday within a month. Hover over it to hold it still; when the next event is soon, it stays on that. It steps aside while you type.
- **Moon and holidays:** the moon's phase is in the **This week** bubble, beside the sun's day. Holidays (plus the days the clocks change) are marked on the month calendar. US holidays are built in; elsewhere they come from Nager.Date for the country your browser's language names. Each holiday has its own look (Halloween is orange with a pumpkin, Christmas wears a Santa hat). On the month calendar a holiday's day looks like any other except for its emblem in the corner, which wiggles when you point at it or click the day; its name shows on the Today card when you click it. Settings → Calendar picks which ones show; a few more, like Super Bowl Sunday and Election Day, start out off.
- **Deliveries** has its own card above Unread: your shipping emails from the last two weeks, read or not, each read in full for the tracking number, the carrier, the day it's due and what's in the box (with its picture when the email has one; otherwise the store's logo). Gmail is read through Gmail's own page, the one you'd open yourself, with the sign-in already in Chrome: Daybook opens it out of sight in the new tab, searches for shipping emails and reads each one's printable page, about every 20 minutes. No Google API and nothing to set up, and nothing in your mailbox changes. Outlook is searched through the Outlook sign-in the Unread card uses. Forwarded emails count as the store's (an order forwarded from Outlook to Gmail shows once), and emails about one order are put together by tracking or order number. When an email has a UPS, USPS, FedEx, DHL or Amazon tracking number, the link goes to the carrier's tracking page; otherwise it opens the email.
- **Package tracking:** each package with a tracking number, from a shipping email or added by hand in Settings → Mail and deliveries (**Track it**, with a name for it), gets its status, latest word and delivery day from the carrier's own site, free and without a key: Daybook opens its tracking page at UPS, USPS, FedEx, DHL or Amazon out of sight, in the new tab, and reads it the way you would, a few packages at a time, every couple of hours (every half hour while one's out for delivery). When a page can't be read, the newest shipping email that mentions the number stands in; failing that, the package still links to the carrier's page. Untick **Find packages in shipping emails too** to show only the packages you add, or **Check carriers' tracking pages** to stop the lookups. To hear about every package coming to your address without typing anything in, sign up free for UPS My Choice, FedEx Delivery Manager and USPS Informed Delivery: they email you as each package moves, and Daybook reads those emails. Carriers change their pages now and then, so a lookup can stop working until Daybook catches up.
- **Site groups** start the shortcuts row (hidden for now), like folders on a bookmarks bar. Hover over a group's icon (the hammer for **Hardware**: Lowe's, Home Depot, Tractor Supply) and its sites pop out as bubbles; click one to go there. **Edit** at the end of the bubbles renames the group, changes its icon and adds or removes sites. Add more groups in Settings → Shortcuts and notes. Groups sync with your Chrome profile.
- **Companies:** a page for each company you deal with (open it from Settings → Shortcuts and notes). Keep the phone number, your account number, hours, the people you talk to, notes and links there; **Look up on Wikipedia** adds a short summary. Each page lists the email and deliveries Daybook has seen from that company, matched by its website or email domain. Companies you get email from are suggested under **From your inbox**. Company pages sync with your Chrome profile.
- **Markets** (optional): add a free Finnhub key and your symbols in Settings → Markets. Quotes refresh every 5 minutes while the US market is open; click a row to open it on Yahoo Finance.
- **Teams** (optional): follow teams in Settings → Teams (NFL, college football, NBA, WNBA, men's college basketball, MLB, NHL or MLS). The Teams card shows each team's next game, with both teams' logos, the date, time and TV channel, the score while a game is on, the record and the last result, plus recent team news. Their games also show on the Today list with the team's logo, and on the month calendar as the two teams' logos (vs for home games, @ for away; hover for the names); click one for its details and a link to ESPN. For a day after a team you follow plays, the result (both teams' logos and the score, green for a win and red for a loss) takes a turn in the bubble at the end of the search bar, and the card leaves it out of the team's last result; a win also stays in the ticker. A game since then ends it early. Schedules come from ESPN and refresh every few hours, every 15 minutes on game day and every 2 minutes during a game. Games, news and the score in the search bar can each be turned off in Settings → Teams.
- **Around you** lists concerts, shows, festivals and games near your weather location for the next two weeks, grouped by day with a picture, the venue and the time; click one to open it on Ticketmaster. It needs a free Ticketmaster key: sign up at developer.ticketmaster.com, open **My Apps** and paste the **Consumer Key** in Settings → Around you, where you can also choose how far, how far ahead and which kinds of events. Small local happenings like brewery nights are rarely on Ticketmaster, so the card also has quick **Brewery events**, **Festivals** and **This weekend** searches for your town, which work without a key.
- **NYS jobs** lists new vacancies open to the general public on StateJobsNY (statejobs.ny.gov), New York State's jobs site, grouped by the day they were posted, newest first, with the agency, county, grade, salary and application deadline; click one to open the posting. Hover over a posting and click the **eye** to mark it as reviewed and take it off the card (Undo is at the bottom); the list is kept on this computer. The card shows as many as fit; the count next to the title is how many there are, and **See all** opens a page with every one of them as a table: every column of the site's vacancy list plus the salary and employment type. There you can search them, click a column heading to sort by it (again to reverse), look at other days, or untick **Only postings that pass my rules** to see everything. **Rules** (on the card, or Settings → NYS jobs) has every option of the site's own search, in the same order: *Containing the keyword*, *With the title*, *In the jurisdictional class*, *In which NYS agency*, *NY HELPS*, *55-b/c Eligible* and *Internship*; *Restrict by date posted* (today's or yesterday's postings, the last few days, or between two dates); *Flexibility in the workplace* (flextime, compressed workweek, telecommuting); *Include occupational categories*; *Include regions* (the site's own regions, matched by county; hover over one to see its counties); and *Other requirements*: employment type, salary grade (greater than, less than or equal to a grade level) and minimum salary. Options left empty, and lists with nothing ticked, don't narrow anything. A salary compares where its range starts, which is what new State hires are paid. Under **More rules** you can add your own on any field the site lists, from the vacancy list and from each posting's own page: pick a field, a test (*contains*, *doesn't contain*, *is*, *isn't*, *is at least*, *is at most*, *is more than*, *is less than*) and a value, like *County contains Albany, Saratoga* or *Title doesn't contain Trainee*; values separated by commas match any of them. Settings shows how many of the postings match as you change things. The list refreshes every 2 hours, and Daybook keeps two weeks of postings; each posting's page is read once, three at a time in the background, for the salary, employment type, category and the rest, so those show up a little after new postings do. StateJobsNY has no feed for this, so Daybook reads its pages; if the site changes them, the card says what it can't read.
- **Cards and ticker:** Settings → Cards and ticker lists every card and every part of the top bar (weather, weather alerts, the moon and holidays) with one choice each: on the page only (**Card** or **Top bar**), in the ticker as well (**+ ticker**), in the ticker instead (**Ticker only**), taking turns in the flip card (**Flip card**, cards only), or not at all (**Off**). The same choice sits at the top of each one's own page in Settings, and **Options** beside it jumps there. The ticker is a bar under the top band that crawls through the latest from each, taking turns: today's weather and the days ahead, weather alerts, the moon and the coming holidays, your most visited sites, Today's events still to come, the next two weeks from the month calendar, your notes, quotes, your teams' next games (or scores), headlines led by their news source, NYS jobs, deliveries, unread mail, events around you and new YouTube uploads. With more than one thing in it, each item wears its icon. Hover over the ticker (or tab into it) to hold it still, scroll it with the wheel, a trackpad or the arrow keys, and click anything to open it: an event opens its details, the rest their page. When everything fits, it holds still. If your computer is set to reduce motion, it only moves when you scroll it.
- **Flip card:** tick the cards to flip under Settings → Flip card (or pick **Flip card** for a card under Settings → Cards and ticker) and they share one spot on the board, taking turns: the card turns over to the next every 20 seconds (or 10, 30, a minute, or only when you pick one), holding still while the pointer or the keyboard is on it. Tabs along its top pick one by hand (the left and right arrow keys work too); a card with nothing to show, like Markets without a key, sits out. Move it like any card with **Arrange cards**.
- **Settings** has a page for each part of Daybook, listed down the side (across the top on a narrow window), and a search box that looks through all of them at once.
- Chrome puts the cursor in the address bar on a new tab; press `/` to jump to Daybook's search box.
- Start with `!m` (or make Maps the default search in Settings) and type an address: suggestions appear with a map. Nearest places to where you are come first. Use ↑ ↓ to move between them, Enter to open the highlighted one in Google Maps, or click **Directions**. Places you open are kept as **Recent** places (on this computer only): they show when you click into the search box in Maps mode and at the top while you type. Remove one with its **×**, or use **Clear recent places**. Address suggestions come from Photon (OpenStreetMap data); the map is an embedded Google Map.
- **Ask Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini**: click the circle at the start of the search box to switch it from Google to Claude, then ChatGPT, then Gemini and back (it shows each one's logo, and the one you leave it on stays), or start with `!c`, `!gpt` or `!gem`. The answer drops down in a box under the search box. Without an API key, the box shows the AI's own site, signed in with your account in Chrome, so it answers on your own plan (sign in to each once from Settings → Search and AI, or during first-time setup). The next question you type opens that conversation on the site with your question typed in, ready to send; you can also keep chatting right in the box. With an Anthropic, OpenAI or Gemini API key in Settings → Search and AI, the answer is written into the box instead and follow-ups stay there. Press Esc to close it; Ctrl+Enter or ⌘+Enter always opens the site. Keys stay on this computer and API use is billed by Anthropic, OpenAI or Google.
- In the search box, start with `!y` (YouTube), `!m` (Maps), `!c` (Claude), `!gpt` (ChatGPT), `!gem` (Gemini), `!w` (Wikipedia), `!a` (Amazon) or `!e` (search your Gmail). Ctrl+Enter or ⌘+Enter opens results in a new tab. The engine tabs under the search box are hidden for now.
- Cards show saved data instantly and refresh in the background: Gmail every minute, Outlook every 2, calendars every 5–10, weather alerts every 5, weather every 15, headlines every 20, stocks every 5 while the market is open, NYS jobs every 2 hours. ↻ refreshes everything now. Each feature can be turned off in Settings → Cards and ticker.

## Privacy

Daybook never sends email. It changes mail only when you click **Mark as read** or **Delete** (deleted mail goes to Trash or Deleted Items, so it can be recovered), and it changes calendars only to add an event you fill in or save changes you make to one or delete one you choose (or put things back when you click Undo). Settings sync through your Chrome profile; sign-ins and cached mail, calendar and weather data stay on your computer. The page talks only to Google, Microsoft, Open-Meteo (weather), BigDataCloud (city name from coordinates), the US National Weather Service (alerts for your weather location), Photon (address suggestions in Maps mode and the event form, and looking up the addresses of upcoming events), the FOSSGIS OSRM server (drive times between your location and those addresses), Google Maps (the map preview), Nager.Date (holidays, outside the US), Google's favicon service (logos for your saved sites, companies and deliveries), Wikipedia (only the company name, when you click Look up), the stores whose product pictures appear on the Deliveries card, ESPN (schedules for the teams you follow), StateJobsNY (New York State's list of job vacancies), and, only if you add your own keys, TomTom (traffic), Finnhub (stock quotes), Anthropic, OpenAI and Google's Gemini API (questions you ask Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini on the page), claude.ai, chatgpt.com and gemini.google.com (the site itself, signed in as you, when you ask one of them without a key), Ticketmaster (events near your weather location), and the carriers' tracking pages at UPS, USPS, FedEx, DHL and Amazon (the tracking numbers of your packages, opened in your browser like a visit of your own). Full policy: https://gr00gles.github.io/privacy.html

## Updating

**Your own copy (from GitHub):** open GitHub Desktop, choose the `daybook` repository and click **Fetch origin**, then **Pull origin**. The next new tab has the changes. If it shows **Finish update**, click it once and open a new tab.

**Copies you shared as a zip:** send a new zip; the person replaces the files in their `daybook` folder and opens a new tab (clicking **Finish update** if it appears). Sign-ins and settings are kept.
